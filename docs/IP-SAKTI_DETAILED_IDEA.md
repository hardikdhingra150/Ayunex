# IP-SAKTI Sahayak
## A source-grounded guide for Ayurveda innovation

**Team:** Naut IQ  
**SIH Problem Statement:** 26045  
**Organisation:** Ministry of Ayush — All India Institute of Ayurveda

> This document describes the proposed idea, not the current implementation. All capabilities below are intended features. IP-SAKTI provides information and preparation support, not legal advice, regulatory approval or a guarantee of intellectual-property protection.

## 1. The idea in one sentence

IP-SAKTI Sahayak is a multilingual, evidence-grounded assistant that helps an Ayurveda innovator understand their product, explore possible intellectual-property protection, identify regulatory and biodiversity questions, and prepare the next steps—with the relevant jurisdiction, official sources and uncertainties always visible.

## 2. The problem we want to solve

An Ayurveda idea does not face just one question. A researcher may want to protect a formulation, a startup may want to sell a botanical product, and a cultivator may want to understand responsibilities connected to biological resources. Each person can encounter several different systems at once.

They may need to understand:

- What kind of product they are developing and which regulatory route could apply.
- Whether an aspect of the idea may be eligible for intellectual-property protection.
- Whether existing traditional knowledge or other prior art affects that possibility.
- Which questions arise from the identity, origin and use of biological resources.
- How proposed claims, labels and advertisements affect the product's position.
- What changes when the intended market moves from India to another country.
- Which authority, record or professional can help them take the next step.

The information is spread across legislation, rules, official registries, treaties, regulatory materials and decisions. Finding a document is only the beginning: a user must also understand its scope, effective date and relationship to their particular facts.

A generic chatbot may provide a fluent answer while overlooking a decisive fact or mixing different legal contexts. A search engine may provide useful links without explaining how to connect them. The proposed solution addresses the space between those two experiences.

## 3. The central insight

**Begin with the product and its facts—not with a premature legal conclusion.**

“Can I patent this?” is often not the first question that needs answering. It may be necessary to establish the intended use, ingredients, formula source, modifications, applicant circumstances and target market before useful guidance can be given.

At the same time, three questions must remain separate:

1. **Regulation:** What product category and associated requirements might apply?
2. **Intellectual property:** What aspects might be protectable, and what limitations or prior knowledge need examination?
3. **Biological resources:** What access, origin and benefit-sharing questions need resolution?

A regulatory category does not establish patentability. A possible patent opportunity does not establish permission to sell. Neither establishes biodiversity compliance.

IP-SAKTI brings these questions into one case while keeping their conclusions distinct.

## 4. Who the assistant is for

| User | Typical need |
| --- | --- |
| AYUSH startup or MSME | Understand a proposed product, protection options and questions to resolve before launch. |
| Researcher or student innovator | Explore prior knowledge, document possible novelty and prepare for professional IP review. |
| Ayurveda practitioner | Understand the issues surrounding a formulation, its intended use and proposed claims. |
| Cultivator or biological-resource supplier | Identify which origin, sourcing and activity facts may matter for biodiversity-related duties. |
| Traditional-knowledge holder or community organisation | Understand possible protection pathways and concerns about inappropriate disclosure or use. |
| IP facilitator or institutional support centre | Receive a structured case with facts, evidence and unresolved questions instead of an unorganised conversation. |

The assistant should adapt the explanation to the user's familiarity without changing the underlying evidence or hiding uncertainty.

## 5. What the user receives

The output is not simply a long chat response. It is a **source-linked case brief** containing:

- A concise description of the product or research question.
- The facts used and the facts still missing.
- A provisional regulatory route, alternative routes or an unresolved result.
- A separate discussion of potential IP pathways.
- A separate set of biological-resource and traditional-knowledge checkpoints.
- Relevant regulatory, evidence, labelling and advertising questions.
- The selected jurisdiction and the date for which guidance is requested.
- Citations supporting material statements.
- An actionable preparation checklist.
- Issues that should be referred to a qualified human professional.

The user should leave knowing what to investigate next, what information to collect and what cannot yet be concluded.

## 6. Two ways to begin

### A. Ask a general question

A user asking “What is a trademark?” or “What does prior art mean?” should not be forced to complete a product form.

The assistant asks only for context needed to answer safely, such as the relevant jurisdiction. If the question turns into a product-specific assessment, it explains why additional facts are necessary.

### B. Explore a particular product

A user with a formulation or commercial idea creates a Product Passport. This becomes the shared factual basis for subsequent guidance.

Both entry points lead to source-grounded information, but only the product-specific route requires a detailed product profile.

## 7. The Product Passport

The Product Passport is a guided record of facts that can affect the assessment. It is not a registration, licence or official certificate.

It may capture:

- Intended use and proposed product claims.
- Dosage or product form and route of administration.
- Formula source or reference to an authoritative text, where relevant.
- Ingredients, biological identities and parts used.
- Known origin and sourcing circumstances of biological resources.
- Processing, extraction, isolation, standardisation or formulation changes.
- Available quality, safety or effectiveness evidence.
- Applicant or organisation circumstances relevant to the assessment.
- Development and commercial stage.
- Intended Indian and overseas markets.
- Previous publications, public disclosures or filings.

### How the questioning should feel

The assistant asks a small group of related questions at a time. It explains why a sensitive or important fact is requested and skips irrelevant branches.

“I do not know” is a valid answer. The assistant must not pressure users into guessing. Unknown facts remain visible and may prevent a conclusion.

Exact confidential formulation ratios are not requested by default. Users should be able to understand the service and its privacy conditions before deciding what to disclose.

Facts extracted from a document or voice input must be presented for confirmation before they influence an assessment. The record should distinguish user-entered, extracted and human-confirmed information.

## 8. Understanding the regulatory route

The assistant considers the product categories identified in the problem statement, including classical or text-based medicines, patent-or-proprietary medicines, relevant non-classical or new-drug routes, phytopharmaceuticals, Ayurveda Aahara or other applicable food routes, and cosmetics.

These are possible routes to investigate—not labels assigned from a single keyword. A botanical ingredient alone does not determine a product's category. Intended use, claims, composition, processing and applicable definitions may all matter.

For a possible route, the assistant explains:

- Which recorded facts point toward it.
- Which conditions appear satisfied, unsatisfied or unknown.
- What evidence supports that interpretation.
- Which missing or contradictory facts could change it.
- Whether an alternative route needs consideration.
- Which authority or professional should resolve the uncertainty.

The assistant may conclude that a product is outside supported scope or cannot yet be classified. That is a valid outcome, not a failure to produce an answer.

## 9. Exploring intellectual-property options

The assistant should consider the part of the innovation the user wants to protect, rather than assuming that every case is a patent application.

Possible areas for investigation include:

- Patents for potentially eligible technical inventions.
- Trademarks for distinctive names or identifiers.
- Geographical indications where the relevant collective and geographical conditions may apply.
- Designs for eligible visual features.
- Copyright for eligible original expression.
- Trade-secret approaches for information that may appropriately remain confidential.
- Plant-variety protection where the subject matter and applicable conditions warrant examination.

The assistant explains the purpose and boundaries of these pathways and identifies the facts or searches needed for further evaluation. It does not promise that an application will succeed.

### Traditional knowledge and prior art

Existing traditional knowledge must remain visible in the analysis. The assistant should help the user identify relevant prior-art search directions and official resources, including appropriate TKDL pointers.

It must not imply unrestricted access to restricted databases. Nor should it claim that a preliminary search establishes novelty or that the absence of a retrieved result proves that no prior art exists.

A useful outcome may be a better-defined research question or a different protection strategy, rather than an unsupported recommendation to pursue a patent.

## 10. The biodiversity and ABS helper

The access-and-benefit-sharing helper organises the facts needed to investigate biological-resource obligations.

Relevant questions may concern:

- The resource and its biological identity.
- Its known origin and sourcing circumstances.
- Who is accessing or using it.
- The activity being undertaken.
- The intended research or commercial purpose.
- Any associated traditional knowledge.
- Applicable permissions, exceptions or unresolved conditions requiring authoritative review.

The output is a set of source-supported checkpoints, not a blanket compliance declaration. Where applicability depends on uncertain facts or interpretation, the assistant explains the dependency and recommends review.

This assessment remains independent of the product's regulatory category and possible IP protection.

## 11. A clear jurisdiction boundary

The user explicitly selects the legal context:

- **India:** Indian IP, product-regulatory and biodiversity questions.
- **International treaty framework:** The relevant international instruments and their scope.
- **A specific target market:** Country- or region-specific questions within supported coverage.

An international treaty explanation must not be presented as if it were a target country's complete domestic law. Guidance for India must not be silently combined with export-market guidance.

Every answer carries its jurisdiction and applicable date. Changing the jurisdiction, date or decisive product facts requires a fresh assessment; the assistant must not simply relabel an old answer.

Language selection is separate. An English query can concern India, and a Hindi query can concern an overseas market.

## 12. Evidence that the user can inspect

Every material legal or regulatory statement should be traceable to the authority relied upon.

A useful citation includes:

- Authority and source title.
- Specific section, rule, article, form or record locator.
- The supporting original passage.
- Jurisdiction.
- Source version and effective date or interval, where known.
- Retrieval date and answer's as-of date.
- Official link where access is permitted.
- Any relevant review or access limitation.

The user should be able to open a citation beside the statement it supports. A link to an authority's homepage alone is not sufficient evidence for a detailed legal conclusion.

Where translation is provided, the original passage remains available and unchanged. If an effective date or source status cannot be established, the assistant says so.

## 13. Making uncertainty useful

The assistant should communicate evidence support through understandable states:

| State | Meaning for the user |
| --- | --- |
| Supported in scope | Available evidence supports the specific statement within the stated context; this is not a guarantee of legal correctness. |
| Missing facts | More decisive information about the case is needed. |
| Missing evidence | Adequate authoritative support has not been established. |
| Conflicting sources | Relevant sources or interpretations need reconciliation. |
| Out of scope | The question or jurisdiction is not covered sufficiently. |
| Review required | A qualified human should resolve the remaining issue. |

These states are preferable to an unexplained confidence percentage. The assistant explains what is uncertain and what could resolve it.

If only part of a question can be supported, it answers that part while keeping the remaining sections explicitly unresolved. It should never fill missing authority with plausible-sounding text.

## 14. Multilingual access

The initial experience should support English and Hindi, with scope to expand to additional Indian languages.

Users should be able to understand questions, review their facts and read plain-language explanations in their preferred language. A short glossary can explain unfamiliar terms without overwhelming the main answer.

Optional voice interaction can improve access for users who prefer speaking. However, botanical names, quantities, negation and other decisive details require confirmation after transcription.

Translation must not change source identifiers, official links or the legal context. Equivalent confirmed facts should lead to consistent assessments regardless of the language used to enter them.

## 15. Moving from an answer to action

The assistant helps the user prepare for real-world follow-up by organising:

- Documents and facts still required.
- Relevant official registries or authority pages.
- Questions for an IP facilitator or regulatory professional.
- Prior-art search directions.
- Open sourcing and biodiversity questions.
- Evidence and claim-related issues to review before further action.

It may point to an appropriate official form or process, but should not represent a draft checklist as a completed filing or granted approval.

The case can be exported as a readable brief or structured record. Each export preserves the facts, jurisdiction, date, cited sources, missing information and disclaimer used for that version.

## 16. Human facilitator handoff

When the case needs professional interpretation, the user can prepare a review packet instead of starting again from an unstructured conversation.

The packet contains the user's question, confirmed facts, separate assessments, source references and unresolved issues. The user reviews the sharing scope and explicitly consents before anything is delivered.

The distinction between a prepared packet, a submitted request and an actual reviewer response must be clear. The assistant must never imply that a professional has reviewed a case when no such review occurred.

Human review is part of the proposed service, not an admission that the assistant has failed. Its role is to make that review more focused and better informed.

## 17. Privacy and responsible access

The service should be designed around minimal disclosure and user control.

- Collect only information needed for the active question.
- Explain storage and processing before collecting sensitive facts.
- Separate consent for case processing, voice processing and facilitator sharing.
- Do not treat submitted formulations as material for a shared public knowledge collection.
- Respect access restrictions and licensing conditions of source databases.
- Access a user's paid subscription only with explicit, recorded permission and within its allowed terms.
- Offer clear controls for accessing, exporting and requesting deletion of personal data.
- Distinguish a temporary demonstration from a securely stored real case.

The proposal does not claim legal or security compliance merely because these controls are planned. They must be established and assessed before real sensitive use.

## 18. Illustrative user journeys

The following examples illustrate the experience, not legal outcomes.

### Journey A — An Ayurveda startup with a capsule concept

The startup asks whether its capsule can be patented. The assistant first records its intended use, formula reference, ingredients, modifications and disclosure history.

It separates the product-category question from possible IP protection and resource-related duties. If ingredient origin is unknown, that uncertainty remains visible. If prior-art research is incomplete, patentability remains unresolved.

The startup receives a case brief showing which facts and source searches are needed before an IP facilitator can give a useful opinion.

### Journey B — A botanical cosmetic concept

The user describes a topical product and proposed claims. The assistant explores the relevant product-route questions without assuming that a botanical ingredient makes the product an Ayurveda medicine.

It also identifies questions around the brand, any potentially protectable innovation and biological-resource sourcing. If the claims create ambiguity, the assistant asks for clarification rather than selecting a category prematurely.

### Journey C — A researcher asking a general IP question

The researcher asks for a plain-language explanation of prior art. The assistant answers within the chosen jurisdiction and provides supporting references without demanding a Product Passport.

If the researcher later asks about a specific unpublished invention, the assistant explains the privacy considerations and requests only the facts needed for the next step.

### Journey D — An innovator considering an export market

The user keeps the India case and opens a separate supported target-market context. The assistant identifies new questions for that market and clearly distinguishes treaty-level information from domestic requirements.

It does not treat an Indian product position or IP filing as proof that the product may be sold abroad.

## 19. What makes the proposal distinctive

### Product-led rather than chat-led

A structured Product Passport provides a consistent basis for guidance and reduces repeated clarification.

### Separate assessments within one case

Regulatory, IP and biodiversity questions are connected without being collapsed into a single verdict.

### Evidence beside the statement

The user can inspect the supporting passage, provision and source context rather than relying on a fluent explanation alone.

### Visible limits

Missing facts, unsupported jurisdictions and conflicting evidence are first-class outcomes with clear next steps.

### Language access without changing meaning

The interface adapts to the user while preserving confirmed facts, source identity and jurisdiction.

### Preparation for human decisions

The output is a reusable case brief that helps the user engage with authorities and professionals more effectively.

## 20. Expected value

For innovators, the proposed service could reduce time spent locating the right starting points and help avoid pursuing a protection or commercialisation route on incomplete assumptions.

For facilitators, it could improve the quality of incoming cases by collecting relevant facts and identifying specific unresolved issues.

For institutions and the wider Ayurveda ecosystem, it could support better-informed innovation while keeping traditional knowledge, resource responsibilities and evidence visible.

These are intended benefits, not demonstrated results. Their extent would need to be measured through user studies, expert review and real case outcomes.

## 21. Boundaries of the idea

IP-SAKTI is not:

- A replacement for an IP professional, lawyer, regulator or qualified scientific reviewer.
- A patentability guarantee or automatic grant of rights.
- A regulatory licence, product approval or ABS clearance.
- A diagnostic or treatment assistant.
- An unrestricted gateway into TKDL or paid databases.
- A system that treats every retrieved document as current and applicable.
- A reason to disclose confidential knowledge before understanding the consequences.

Its purpose is to make the path to a sound decision clearer—not to remove the need for authoritative decisions.

## 22. What success would look like

The idea succeeds when a user can:

1. Describe their question or product without needing specialist terminology.
2. Understand which facts are decisive and which remain unknown.
3. Distinguish product regulation, IP protection and biological-resource obligations.
4. Inspect the authority behind material statements.
5. Recognise the limits of the answer and the selected jurisdiction.
6. Leave with a useful checklist or a focused request for human review.

Success should be judged by answer accuracy, citation support, appropriate abstention, multilingual consistency and user understanding—not merely by response speed or visual polish.

## 23. Final vision

IP-SAKTI Sahayak aims to become a trusted starting point for people taking Ayurveda ideas forward: accessible enough for a first-time innovator, structured enough for a facilitator, and careful enough to keep evidence and uncertainty visible.

**The promise is clarity—not automatic approval: understand the product, respect its knowledge and resources, inspect the evidence, and take the next informed step.**
